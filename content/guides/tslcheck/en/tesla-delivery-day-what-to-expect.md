Delivery day is the one part of buying a Tesla almost nobody has practiced. You've researched
the car for weeks, maybe months — but the actual sequence of events at the delivery center is
usually a surprise the first time, and that unfamiliarity is exactly what causes people to
rush through the one moment that's easiest to fix problems in. This is a step-by-step roadmap
so you walk in knowing what's coming.

**TL;DR — the sequence:**

1. Notification your car is ready, often with little notice
2. Arrival and paperwork/ID check
3. A supervised walkthrough of the car with a delivery advisor
4. Time (sometimes limited) to inspect the car yourself
5. A short test drive near the delivery center
6. Signing and accepting the car
7. Driving off

The whole point of this guide is step 4 — the part that's easy to rush and hardest to redo.

## Before you arrive

**You'll usually get notice a few days out, sometimes less.** Delivery windows can move, and
last-minute date changes are common — build a little slack into your schedule if you can, and
don't plan anything immediately after that requires you to rush the appointment.

**Decide your inspection plan before you get there,** not while you're standing next to the
car. Deciding *at* the delivery center what to look at wastes the limited time you have there
on decision-making instead of actually looking. A written or app-based checklist means you're
executing a plan, not improvising one.

## What actually happens at the delivery center

**Paperwork and ID first.** Expect a short administrative step — confirming your identity,
signing initial paperwork, sometimes a walkthrough of financing or insurance details if those
weren't finalized online.

**A guided walkthrough with an advisor.** Someone will typically show you the car's basics —
opening doors and the frunk/trunk, charging, the touchscreen — partly onboarding, partly a
chance for them to note the car looks good. This is not the same thing as your own inspection;
treat it as the intro, not the main event.

**Your own inspection time.** This is the part that varies most by location and how busy the
day is. Some delivery centers give you generous time; others move faster than you'd like.
Either way, this is the moment to work through your plan methodically rather than glancing
around — see the sections below for what to prioritize if time is tight.

## If your time is limited: what to prioritize

If you only have a few minutes, work in this order — it's roughly ranked by how often each
issue is reported and how much harder each gets to fix after you leave:

1. **Panel gaps** — walk the car comparing each gap side to side (doors, hood/frunk, trunk).
2. **Screen and warnings** — a bright, uniform image on the touchscreen to spot dead pixels;
   confirm no persistent alerts.
3. **Lights** — check for condensation inside headlight and tail light housings.
4. **Paint** — a quick pass in direct light for scratches, chips, or mismatched panels.
5. **Interior trim** — press gently along trim edges for anything not fully clipped in.

If you have more time, extend to seals, glass, and accessories — but these five cover the
issues buyers most often report.

## The test drive

Tesla delivery typically includes a short, supervised drive near the delivery center before
you finalize acceptance. Use those few minutes with intent rather than just enjoying the car:

- Listen for any rattle or wind noise, especially at higher speed if the route allows it
- Test the brakes gently at low speed for any pulling or unusual feel
- Confirm the steering feels centered and responsive
- Try the drive modes if you have time

This is your one chance to catch a functional issue before you're driving it home alone.

## Signing and driving off

Before you sign, do one last pass in your head: **is everything you found written down
somewhere with a date on it?** A note from the advisor, an email, or simply your own photos
with a visible timestamp all work — what matters is that the record doesn't depend only on
your memory of the conversation. Once you've confirmed that, the safest moment for
documentation has passed the instant you drive away, so it's worth the extra sixty seconds.

## The through-line: a plan beats improvising

Every piece of advice in this guide points at the same thing: delivery day goes better when
you're executing a plan instead of making decisions on the spot, under time pressure, with an
advisor who's done this a hundred times today and you who's doing it for the first time. That
gap in experience is exactly what a structured checklist closes — which is the whole reason
TSL Check exists as a free, guided walkthrough you can bring with you instead of trying to
remember what to check.
